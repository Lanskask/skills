# Content philosophy (video edition)

Same principles as `book-to-course`; the learner is learning _from a talk_, not a book. Read this when writing the
outline (Pass 1) and each lesson (Pass 3).

- **Visual first.** At most 2-3 sentences in a row. Lists of 3+ become `cards`, sequences `steps`, the speaker's key
  sentence a `quote` with a plain-language `meaning`.
- **Speaker ↔ plain words** replaces code ↔ English: left side is a short verbatim quote with its timestamp link,
  right side says it the way you would to a friend. At least one per module.
- **One concept per screen.** If a screen needs two headings, make two screens.
- **Metaphors first, then reality.** A fresh metaphor per lesson, never restaurant/kitchen, never repeated. The metaphor
  is yours; it carries no timestamp.
- **Define for a beginner.** First use of a term: `[[term]]` plus a glossary entry for a student who knows little yet.
  Tie it to something the learner can run, plot or check.
- **Restructure, don't transcribe.** A talk wanders; a course does not. Group by idea, not by minute, and say in the
  outline when you reorder. Timestamps let the learner jump back to the original.
- **Quizzes test application**, not recall: a new scenario, plausible wrong options, an explanation for each outcome.
- **Be honest about the source.** Speakers oversimplify, sell, or are outdated: say it in a `warning` callout. Say when
  the transcript is uncertain (auto-captions, Whisper).
- **Quote sparingly.** ≤ 25 words per quote, ≤ 15% of a lesson, always attributed (the renderer adds speaker, video
  title and a ▶ link).
- **Arc.** Motivation → core idea → mechanism → worked consequence → limits. 1-6 modules for one video; one module per
  video for a playlist; 2-6 lessons per module.
- **Visuals.** Diagrams are drawn from specs (flow, plot) and labelled "made for this course"; the video's own picture
  is never reproduced. Use a diagram when the idea is a process, a loop, or a dependence.
- **What the video showed but never said.** If the transcript says "look at this graph", you cannot describe the graph.
  Either use the `[on-screen]` OCR text (if `--frames` was run) or tell the learner to watch that moment (link it).
